# P2 on the AWS F2: the lane memory (M4), the F2 target (M5), the build

Branch `p2-f2` (from `e04/v4-p2-four-lanes` 72eb4b4c, P2's M1-M2: [p2-status.md](p2-status.md)).
Proposal: [proposal-2-four-lanes.md](proposal-2-four-lanes.md). Every number below is a receipt on
the m8a build box (54.174.195.152) with its file named; nothing here ran on an F2 card. Labels:
"RTL" = Verilator, the whole model's decode token (26 layers + the 262,144-row int8 head) at
position 14 after the 15-token chat prompt, compared with the ISA simulator (logits and the DRAM
image bit for bit); the memory model is named on every line.

## M4: the lane memory (an image copy per lane)

**Decision.** Each of the four lock-step chunk lanes reads its own copy of the slice's DRAM image,
copy l on its own pair of HBM channels behind its own two-channel adapter (`otpu_native_dram`,
unchanged: the adapter of Alex's burst200 image that ran Gemma 3 1B on the card, and the one P2's
M2 gate ran lane 0 through). Every write (port SW's QSTs, port B's DMA stores) goes to all four
copies, taken in the cycle every copy can take it; reads go to the reader's copy (lane 0 and the
DMA on copy 0). `rtl/mem/otpu_lane_mem.sv`.

Why not one image over all channels (the proposal's 32-PC adapter, or the wide-port-alexshell
adapter at 8 channels with three more read ports): the lanes read rows n + l of every MM at any
row stride, so on one shared image their chunks collide on channels and come back out of order.
That needs a 4 x 8 crossbar, a reorder buffer per lane and a response FIFO per channel and source
(4x the wide-port adapter's LUT-RAM, which is what congested SLR2 in the alexshell builds:
docs/wide_port.md "Alex's shell at 150 MHz, placed"). With a copy per lane each lane has a plain
in-order adapter and its own channels: no crossbar, no reordering, timing local to a lane, and a
channel streams its lane's consecutive chunks (a row of 9 chunks = 9 consecutive beats a channel:
bursts form, unlike the 8-channel hash where a channel saw one beat at a time). It scales to P3's
eight lanes (16 channels, 8 copies).

What it costs: the image four times (Gemma 3 1B int8 ~1.05 GB a copy, 8 GiB of the 16 GiB HBM
reserved), the host writes every copy (four times the upload; `opentpu/host/f2.py`), and a write
occupies every copy's channels (the KV appends and the logits store: ~1.1 MB a token).

Ordering is the adapter's, per copy (otpu_native_dram "Ordering"): wr_idle and rd_idle are every
copy's, a_flush every copy's. Simulation checks the copies stay equal: each copy's model prints
its image's hash at the dump and `rtlsim` fails the run if they differ.

## M5: the F2 target and the memory path

**The fabric: wide-port-alexshell's, at P2's geometry.** The eight channels (4 copies x 2) take
the alexshell path unchanged: per channel the 3-stage SLR register chain (otpu_xr, SLR2 -> SLR1 ->
SLR0), `otpu_native_axi` (Alex's adapter: read bursts, direction barriers), AWS's 512-bit register
slice, a 1 x 1 SmartConnect (clock and width conversion), AWS's 256-bit register slice at 450 MHz,
the HBM AXI port; the host's PCIS through the register gate, AWS's chain and its own HBM port;
the build flow (`boards/f2/build.py`: SynthCL / ImplCL / BuildAll --package, qualification,
route_opt.py) and the floorplan. The burst200 fabric (e04/tpubench) is the on-card-proven one but
at two ports a slice; this path already carries 512 B/cycle for one slice and its 150 MHz build
placed at +0.089 ns (routed -0.064 ns from SLR2 congestion of the wide-port slice's LUT-RAM
adapter).

**Channel windows of 1 GiB.** A copy needs ~1.05 GB, more than two 512 MiB pseudo-channels, so a
channel is a 1 GiB window of two pseudo-channels: channel c at HBM [c GiB, (c + 1) GiB) through
HBM AXI port 2c (its own pseudo-channel directly, the other through the port's mini-switch at full
rate), the host on port 15 through the global switch. `targets/f2-p2.toml` (channel_bytes 1 GiB,
hbm_port_stride 2, hbm_host_port 15), `build.py hbm_ports`, the CL's port map and tie-offs
(`boards/f2/design/cl_opentpu_hbm.sv` PSTRIDE / HPORT), `create_hbm_sc.tcl` (the windows), the
floorplan's port boxes. A copy's DRAM is 2 GiB (`DRAM_BYTES`, CFG_HASH a09812d1).

**CFG registers.** CFG_DRAM [31:24] = copies - 1 (two channels a copy reported in [15:8]),
CFG_UARCH [28:25] = MXU_C - 1 (both 0 on every bitstream before). `opentpu/host/regs.py cfg`.

**SWFILL.** The F2's cl_hbm runs with ECC correction off, so it takes byte-masked writes at full
rate; `otpu_native_dram SWFILL 0` (set by otpu_f2_hbm_sys) writes SW partial beats (a transposed V
column: one byte a beat) with their masks instead of a fill read first, which on the F2 path cost
a read / write direction change in u_naxi per 32 beats (see "Receipts").

**Host.** `opentpu/host/f2.py` is e04/tpubench's F2 transport (the card path of the burst200 builds,
platform 3) with the copies: `mem_write` writes every copy, `mem_read` reads copy 0, the Board sees
one copy's two channels; `OTPU_DEVICE=f2:<slot>` selects it (board.open_transport).
tests/test_f2_copies.py.

## Clock: 150 MHz first

The alexshell slice (one MXU of MCOLS 8 with QUAD, 1,275 DSPs in SLR2) placed at -1.078 ns on the
core clock at 200 MHz and closed placement only at 150 MHz; P2's slice carries four MCOLS 4 arrays
(~2,200 DSPs, ~73% of SLR2's) with the lanes' lock step (pop_ok, the 4096-bit ACT broadcast)
spanning them. 150 MHz (TMEM pumped at 300) is the first target (`f2-p2:c150`); c175 and c200 are
variants for when it closes with margin.

**What the F2 path costs, and the three fixes.** The LPN puts the path's latency (58 cycles
against 30) at 0.2% of the token, so the 15% of the first F2-path receipt is the path's structure.
otpu_naxi_mem's counters (u_naxi as built: burst 8, waiting, 32 transactions, the full direction
barrier) per channel: ~440K bursts for 2.11M beats (152K of one beat, 170K of eight), a write
at the queue's head waiting for the reads in flight 44-56K cycles, reads waiting for write
responses 10-12K, 830-1000 write episodes (logs/p2-tok-bar-sw1.log).

- **SWFILL 0** (rtl/mem/otpu_native_dram.sv): the F2's cl_hbm runs with data masks on and ECC
  correction off (cl_hbm.xci USER_MCn_DM_EN / EN_DATA_MASK true, ENABLE_ECC_CORRECTION false), so
  an SW partial beat (the transposed V's one byte a beat) is written with its mask instead of a
  fill read first; the fill reads' read / write alternation through u_naxi's barrier was the
  quantizer's (QACT at attention.py:117) 5.6% of the token. -72K cycles.
- **Page buckets** (otpu_native_axi PORDER): the barrier per hashed 4 KiB page (32 buckets): a read
  waits only for the unanswered writes of its page's bucket, a write only for the reads in flight
  of its page's bucket; the native contract holds per beat. Write waits halve. -31K cycles.
- **Zero-bubble bursts** (otpu_native_axi ZB): u_naxi spent a cycle without a take closing a burst
  whose next read is not adjacent; a lane's 9-beat rows put that cycle after every row on every
  channel (~267K of the ~437K bursts a channel), at a beat a cycle a channel ~10% of the token. With
  ZB the head opens the next burst while the open one goes to the free AR register. -200K cycles:
  the F2 path now costs +1.7% over the native model at LAT 30 (2,340,337 against 2,301,223).

The build (`otpu_f2_hbm_sys`: SWFILL 0, PORDER 1, ZB 1) carries all three.
